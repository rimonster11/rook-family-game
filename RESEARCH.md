## History
- Parker Brothers brought out Rook in 1906. George S. Parker created it with his wife Grace, who picked the name. It was meant for people who objected to standard playing cards and their face cards. [W][RC]
- The 1906–07 catalog sold "Rook Cards" for 50 cents, with rules for ten games (Rook, High 14, Bamboozle, and others). [PB][RC]
- Sales started slowly, but the game built up a following over time. [RC] It is popular in Eastern Kentucky and in Mennonite communities in Ontario, Manitoba, Pennsylvania, and Ohio. [P] Today it is published by Hasbro and Winning Moves. [W]

## Deck
- 57 cards: four color suits (red, yellow, green, black) numbered 1–14, plus the Rook Bird card. [W][P]
- In the main published game, the 1s–4s are removed. That leaves 40 cards plus the Rook, 41 in all. [W][WM]

## Main variants
- **Tournament Rook / "Kentucky Discard"** (official booklet rules): 4 players in two fixed partnerships, partners sitting across. 41 cards, 9 per player, and a 5-card nest dealt one card at a time between rounds of the deal. Minimum bid 70, bids in multiples of 5. Players who pass are out of the bidding. The bidder takes the nest, discards 5 (counters are allowed), and names trump. The player to the dealer's left leads. Game is 300; if both teams pass 300 on the same hand, the higher score wins, and a tie means another hand. [W][WM] Pagat says that in this game the *dealer* bids first. [P] Winning Moves and Wikipedia say the player to the dealer's left bids first. [WM][W]
- **Kentucky Rook (Pagat)**: 1s are kept and rank high (1 = 15 pts). 2–4 are removed (45 cards). 10 cards per player, 5 in the nest. Minimum bid 100, and if the first three players pass, the dealer is forced to bid 100. The bidder may *not* put counters in the nest. The Rook is the highest trump. Game is 500. [P]
- **Kentucky Discard (rookgame.com "Kentucky West-ish")**: 1s rank high (15 pts, 180 total). The Rook is the *lowest* trump. Trump can't be led until someone has trumped in. Taking every point earns a 20-point bonus (200 total). Game is 500. [RG]
- **Partnership Rook** (booklet): full 56-card deck, no nest, no Rook Bird. The side that takes the most tricks gets 20 points. Game is 200. [WM]
- **Call Partner**: Pagat describes a 4-player game with all 57 cards and 13 per player. The bidder names a card, and whoever holds it becomes the bidder's secret partner for that hand. Players keep individual scores and play to 500. [P] rookgame.com describes a 5-player standard-deck version with 10 cards each, a 3-card nest, a called partner, and high/low no-trump options. [RG]
- Other regional games: Western New York, 2-High, Woodson Patrick ("Poker Rook"), and Western Kentucky (the Red 1 is worth 30 and captures the Rook; bids run 70–150). [RG]

## How the Rook card ranks
- Tournament/Kentucky Discard: it is the highest card no matter which suit is trump. It can be played at any time, even when you could follow suit. If it is led, the other players must play trump. You *must* play it when trump is led and it is your only trump. [WM][W][P] The booklet also says it "can be thought of as the equivalent of the 14 of Trump." [WM]
- Pagat's Kentucky Rook: the highest trump, above the 1. Some people play it as the lowest trump instead. [P]
- Call Partner (Pagat): it ranks between the 11 and the 10 of trump (the "10½"). [P]
- rookgame.com Kentucky West-ish, Western NY, and 5-player games: lowest trump, and it must follow suit like any other card. [RG]
- Woodson Patrick: highest trump, but it must follow suit when possible. Western Kentucky: second-highest card, below the Red 1. [RG]

## Bidding conventions
- Bids are multiples of 5 and must be higher than the last bid. A player who passes can't bid again in that hand. Bidding keeps going around until three players have passed. [W][P][WM]
- Minimum bid is 70 in the Tournament game, with 120 as the maximum. [W][WM] Pagat's Kentucky Rook uses 100 (dealer forced to 100). [P] Woodson Patrick runs 65–120 and Western Kentucky runs 70–150, and in some variants a "shoot the moon" bid wins the game outright. [RG]
- Wikipedia's version of the Tournament rules lets a player call for a redeal if their hand has no counters. [W]

## The nest
- Tournament: the nest is dealt during the deal, one card after each round. The high bidder takes it, discards the same number of cards (counters allowed), and names trump. **Whoever wins the last trick takes the nest and scores any counters in it.** [WM][W]
- Pagat's Kentucky Rook does not allow counters in the nest. [P] In Kentucky West-ish, counters may go in only to avoid discarding trump, and the bidder must announce them. [RG] In Western NY, counters in the nest are shown face up. [RG]
- None of my sources mention turning the top nest card face up. That is a house rule; the family rules for this app include it.

## Scoring the counters
- With 1s removed: 5 = 5, 10 = 10, 14 = 10, Rook = 20, for 120 per hand (100 without the Rook). [WM][W]
- With 1s in play: 1 = 15, for 180 per hand. Some variants add 20 for the last trick or for taking every point (200). [P][RG]
- If the bidding team makes its bid, it scores the counters it took. If it falls short, it loses the bid amount, and its score can go negative. The other team always scores its own counters. [WM][W][P]
